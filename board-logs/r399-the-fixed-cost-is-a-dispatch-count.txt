round 399, 2026-09-13 — the 188 ms fixed cost is one chunk's worth of NPU
                        dispatches, and prefill's per-dispatch floor is 11x
                        decode's

r393 fitted charsiu's TTFT to 187.9 + 5.456n + 0.004483n^2 and the vendor's to
45.8 + 6.095n + 0.000743n^2. Their fixed term is a quarter of ours and nothing
had decomposed ours. Section 1e measures milliseconds a ROW and excludes
staging; an intercept is neither.

=====================================================================
1. FIRST, DOES IT EXIST? The fit ran from 27 tokens; 188 is an extrapolation
---------------------------------------------------------------------
r392 threw out an intercept for exactly this reason, so this measures where the
fit did not go.

    tokens   TTFT ms   the fit predicts
         2       167       199
         8       230       232
        16       226       276
        32       313       367
        64       546       555

1) IT EXISTS. A TWO TOKEN PROMPT COSTS 167 ms. The fit over-predicts the
middle -- 276 against 226 at 16 tokens -- so 187.9 is not the right number, but
a fixed cost of about 165 ms is real and is not an artefact of extrapolation.

The short end is not smooth: 167 at 2 tokens, 209 at 3, 171 at 4, 230 at 8,
209 at 12. Chunk width effects (r397) at lengths where the width IS the prompt.

=====================================================================
2. WHAT IT IS MADE OF: hold the rows, vary the chunk count
---------------------------------------------------------------------
One prompt of 160 tokens, every chunk width a multiple of 4 so r397's effect is
held constant, only the number of chunks changing:

    chunks   widths     TTFT ms
         1   1x160         1182
         2   2x80          1120
         4   4x40          1208
         8   8x20          1370
        20   20x8          2430
        40   40x4          2846

2) THE COST IS PER CHUNK, AND IT IS LARGE. Identical rows, identical
arithmetic, and forty chunks cost 2.4x what two do.

3) SO THE INTERCEPT IS A DISPATCH COUNT. A chunk issues one NPU dispatch
per projection per layer plus the head -- 113 on this 16-layer model -- and
that count does not depend on how many rows the chunk carries. A 2-token prompt
is one chunk: 167 ms over 113 dispatches is **about 1.5 ms a dispatch**, which
is the whole of the fixed term.

4) AND THAT IS ELEVEN TIMES THE DECODE FLOOR THIS TREE ALREADY MEASURED.
project-charsiu-call-floor records 130 us a call over 150 calls for decode. At
m = 2 a prefill dispatch does almost exactly what a decode dispatch does, and
costs 1.5 ms against 130 us. That gap is the next question and this round
does not answer it.

=====================================================================
3. AND ONE CHUNK IS NOT THE OPTIMUM, WHICH r397's FIX ASSUMED
---------------------------------------------------------------------
2x80 beats 1x160 by 5.2% above. Sweeping it properly, widen-to-one against
keeping the 80 chunk, every length a multiple of 4 so the residue is held:

    tokens    one    80-chunk   ratio     the tail the 80 chunk leaves
        88    663    737        0.900     8
        96    731    743        0.984     16
       104    771    799        0.965     24
       112    841    835        1.007     32
       120    889    887        1.002     40
       128    974    932        1.045     48
       136    990    991        0.999     56
       144   1042   1076        0.968     64
       152   1125   1095        1.027     72
       160   1186   1114        1.065     80

THE SURFACE IS BUMPY AND TEN POINTS AT TWO REPEATS CANNOT RANK IT. Against a
2% noise floor only 88 (widen wins 10%) and 160 (widen loses 6.5%) are outside
it; 96 through 152 is mostly noise and 144 runs against whatever trend the ends
suggest. This is the shape a coarse grid of a non-smooth parameter has, and no
rule is proposed from it.

WHAT IT DOES SAY: r397's fix -- decline to widen when the width would be
2 mod 4 -- is INCOMPLETE. At 160 the width is 0 mod 4, so the fix lets it
widen, and widening there loses 6.5%. The fix is not wrong, and the 6.8 to 7.3%
it was measured at stands; it does not cover this case.

A mechanism worth testing later, not established here: the alternative to
widening leaves a TAIL, and a tiny tail pays a full 113-dispatch set for very
few rows. 88 tokens leaves a tail of 8 and widening wins biggest there. 160
leaves a tail of 80 and widening loses. 144 leaves 64 and does not fit.
