round 394, 2026-09-13 — the withdrawn attention rule, both halves measured

r393 put charsiu's prefill quadratic term at 0.004483 ms/tok^2 against the
vendor's 0.000743, six times theirs, and said the next question was why. It is
attention, and the answer was already in the tree.

🔑 THE STAGE TABLE SAYS IT DIRECTLY. Llama-3.2-1B, 852 tokens, CPU pinned:

  attention      3.82 ms a row   42.1%    <- the largest row
  gate + up      2.28            25.1%
  down           1.43            15.7%
  q k v          0.59             6.5%
  matmul rows:   4.66 ms a row inside the NPU entry

4.66 is exactly the four projection rows summed (0.59+0.37+2.28+1.43), so
attention's 3.82 ms is OUTSIDE the NPU entry: it runs on the CPU. And 42.1%
measured against 40.2% inferred from r393's quadratic term is two methods
agreeing to two points.

🔑 AND THE KNOB EXISTS AND SAYS WHY IT IS OFF. attn_npu_want_for():

  CHARSIU_ATTN_NPU=auto is withdrawn -- head_dim >= 128 is only half the rule
  and the other half (how long a prompt) has not been measured; attention
  stays on the CPU

This round measures the other half. It also measures the FIRST half, which
nothing in this tree could previously evaluate, because nothing could say which
model was on which side of 128 -- tools/gguf_head_dim.py now does.

=====================================================================
1. NPU attention against CPU attention, by head_dim and prompt length
---------------------------------------------------------------------
ratio = NPU arm / CPU arm, so below 1.00 the NPU arm wins

  model               head_dim   ~52 tok   ~202    ~452    ~852
  Llama-3.2-1B              64     1.269   1.259   1.250   1.257
  Qwen3-0.6B               128     1.622   1.354   1.163   1.018
  gemma-3-1b               256     1.121   1.021   0.923   0.877

🏁 1) BOTH HALVES OF THE RULE ARE REAL. At 852 tokens the ratio is 1.257 at
head_dim 64, 1.018 at 128 and 0.877 at 256: monotone in head_dim. And for the
two models above the threshold the ratio FALLS with prompt length, 1.622->1.018
and 1.121->0.877.

🏁 2) gemma-3-1b WINS FROM ABOUT 230 TOKENS, AND BY 12.3% AT 852. The code
already exists, already agrees with the CPU arm to fp16 rounding, and is off.

🏁 3) THE TWO HALVES ARE NOT INDEPENDENT CONDITIONS, THEY ARE ONE QUANTITY.
head_dim is the INNER dimension of both attention matmuls -- Q is [rows x hd]
against K^T [hd x positions] -- and prompt length is the outer one. Both feed
arithmetic per dispatch, which is what the NPU needs to beat its own fixed
cost. That is why head_dim 64 is FLAT at 1.25 while the others fall: at K=64
no prompt length in range makes a dispatch worth taking.

⛔ 4) SO THE NEGATIVE RESULT ON Llama DOES NOT TEST THE RULE. Llama-3.2-1B is
the model every speed round in this tree uses and it is the FURTHEST BELOW the
threshold of anything on the card. Measuring there and concluding "attention on
the NPU does not pay" would have been the same error as measuring a quantiser
at a group nobody runs.

=====================================================================
2. Correctness, at the LONGEST length of each sweep
---------------------------------------------------------------------
⚠ Checked at the longest length on purpose: an fp16 accumulator's error grows
with how much it accumulates, so a check at a short prompt is the easy case.

  Llama-3.2-1B    worst 0.0086 relative   176 layers, 0 fell back
  Qwen3-0.6B      worst 0.013  relative   308 layers, 0 fell back
  gemma-3-1b      worst 0.0057 relative   286 layers, 0 fell back

On Llama at 202 tokens the generated text is byte-identical between the arms.
⚠ Llama's own worst relative error grew 0.0024 -> 0.0086 from 202 to 852
tokens, which is the reason the check moved to the long end.

=====================================================================
3. A PREDICTION, WRITTEN DOWN FIRST, AND FALSIFIED
---------------------------------------------------------------------
From the three models above, the crossover length L falls roughly as 1/hd^1.9:
never at head_dim 64, about 870 tokens at 128, about 230 at 256. Written down
before gemma-4-E2B ran: head_dim 512 should cross near 60 tokens and reach 0.8
or lower at 852.

  gemma-4-E2B        512     1.105   1.032   0.975   0.936
                             (52)    (202)   (452)   (852)

Crossover about 380 tokens and 0.936 at 852 -- WORSE than head_dim 256, not
better. The 1/hd^2 reading is dead.

⛔ 5) AND SO IS THE ROW ITSELF: gemma-4 IS NOT A head_dim 512 MEASUREMENT.
The tell was in the same output and had to be reached by arithmetic: every
other model ran exactly 11 x n_layer layer-calls on the NPU, and gemma-4 ran
77 = 11 x 7 of an expected 11 x 35. Seven of thirty-five.

The gguf says why:

  gemma4.attention.key_length        512      <- the global layers
  gemma4.attention.key_length_swa    256      <- the sliding-window layers
  gemma4.attention.sliding_window_pattern  <35 items>

**gemma-4 has TWO head_dims.** The pool is built for one, so `hd != a->hd`
refuses all 28 sliding-window layers. gemma-3-1b has a single key_length and
refuses none, which is why all 26 of its layers ran.

⭐ So gemma-4's 0.936 is what SEVEN layers of thirty-five bought. And the
head_dim its other 28 layers want is 256 -- the best-winning head_dim in this
whole sweep.

=====================================================================
4. THE INSTRUMENT SAID "fell back on 0" WHILE 80% OF THE LAYERS LEFT
---------------------------------------------------------------------
`fallbacks` counted the two fp16 matmul failures and nothing else; five earlier
`return -1`s took the CPU path without touching a counter. The number printed
was the number that was easy to count.

Fixed, one counter a reason, with the compound conditions split so each clause
has its own -- head_dim shared a line with the layer index and the kv head
count, so even a counter on that line could not have said which fired:

  gemma-4-E2B   ran 21 layers, fell back on 0, refused 84
                  head_dim     84
  gemma-3-1b    ran 78 layers, fell back on 0, refused 0

21 + 84 = 105 = 3 chunks x 35 layers, and 21 = 3 x 7. The diagnosis above is
now read off the instrument rather than inferred from a multiple of eleven.
