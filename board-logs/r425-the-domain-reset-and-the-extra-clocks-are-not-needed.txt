r425: the NPU0/NPU1 domain reset and the three extra domain clocks are not needed
==================================================================================

2026-11-12, Radxa ROCK 4D, Armbian 26.11.0-trunk (Ubuntu 24.04 desktop),
kernel 7.3.4-edge-rockchip64 (#1 SMP PREEMPT Tue Nov 10 18:10:10 UTC 2026),
which carries v14 of the series: the 15 us settle delay on nputop, npu0 and
npu1, and v14 13/15's domain clocks and BIU reset on npu0 and npu1.

Why: Finley Xiao (Rockchip) replied to v16 10/16 and 14/16 on 2026-11-11.
He says the domain reset is not needed (tested on many RK3576, and the bus
interface is not left undefined after power-on), and that CLK_RKNN_DSU0 and
the two CBUF clocks on PD_NPU0/PD_NPU1 are redundant (DSU0 is the parent of
ACLK_RKNN0/1; the switch flow only needs ACLK_RKNN0/1 and HCLK_RKNN_ROOT).
Where the reset and the clocks came from, read from kernel/ (the series as
exported in June) and the commit history:

  #36-#38  rk_iommu "Error during raw reset. MMU_DTE_ADDR is not
           functioning" on the first runtime resume. SRST_A_RKNN0_BIU sat
           in rknn_core's resets as "srst_h", a slot the RK3576 has no
           reset for.
  #39      kernel/0005 + 0006 (2026-06-10 10:53): pulse the BIU reset from
           the NPU0/NPU1 domain nodes at power-on.
  #40/41   kernel/0007 (16:10): add CLK_RKNN_DSU0 and the two CBUF clocks
           to the domain nodes; its message says "pulsing the BIU reset at
           power-on did not help".
  #42      kernel/0008 (16:24, DEBUG): drop the BIU reset from the domain
           nodes, "the pulse never fixed anything (#36-#38 failed without
           it) and it is an untested variable sitting in the power-on
           path"; add diagnostics.
  #43      kernel/0009 (16:34): the diagnostics show the MMU's DTE_ADDR
           takes writes only while the CBUF clocks run. rk_iommu takes all
           DT clocks and the NPU MMU nodes get the full set. This is the
           fix; it is mainline 841363ebb508, and v16 8/16 measured which
           four clocks the MMU needs.

The reset was dropped by a DEBUG patch, and the exported series left DEBUG
patches out (ca2f3d1), so the drop never reached it and 0005/0006 went on
into v2. v2's message gives a mechanism ("the CRU preserves reset state
across power cycles"); from v3 on it says the NPU hangs on the first access
after a cold power-on and that "the vendor kernel cycles the domain's
resets at this point". Neither has a measurement behind it in this
repository, and the second is false: the vendor kernel
(~/Desktop/rk3576-vendor-kernel, 6.1.115) has no reset_control in
drivers/soc/rockchip/pm_domains.c and no RKNN BIU reset anywhere in its dts
or rknpu driver. Its domain nodes for NPU0/NPU1 list no clocks and no
resets, and its delay table is nputop 15, npu0 0, npu1 0, vop 15 us, which
is what Finley asks for. The domain clocks of 0007 were a hypothesis made
before 0009 found the cause, and were not revisited after it.

Protocol. Only the dtb changes between arms; it is edited with fdtput from
the board's own file. Each arm: install the dtb, reboot (warm), wait, then 30
generations of `charsiu run Qwen3-0.6B-Q4_0 --temp 0 -n 64 "Explain what a
bandgap is."`. The NPU autosuspends after 50 ms, so every generation starts
with npu0 off (checked in pm_genpd_summary before each one) and powers
npu, nputop, npu0 and npu1 up. Per arm: exit codes, the md5 of the output
text with the timing lines removed, and the kernel journal from a marker
for "timed out", rk_iommu/MMU_DTE/page fault, and SError/Internal error.

  A  original dtb                                   md5 1201a529
  B  A with `resets` deleted on power-domain@2 and @3   963d5a4c
  C  A with power-domain@2/@3 clocks back to
     <HCLK_RKNN_ROOT ACLK_RKNN0/1>, resets kept         e8366064

  [A3cold] dtb=1201a529 boot=8630fbd4 runs=30 fail=0 npu0_off_before=30/30 hashes= 30 5e3aaf1a5a57; timeouts=0 iommu=0 serror=0

All 180 outputs have the same text. No arm had a failed run, a job
timeout, an IOMMU error or an SError.

What this shows: on today's code, with the settle delay in place, the BIU
reset and the three extra domain clocks change nothing over 30 domain power
cycles per arm, from a warm reboot or from a power-off, on one board.

What it does not show: one board only; 30 cycles per arm; npu0/npu1 with
the delay removed was not tested (that needs a kernel build); PD_VOP's
delay, which Finley also asks for, was not tested.
