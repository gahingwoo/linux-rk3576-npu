round 417, 2026-09-15 - a per tensor group width, both halves, and a finer
                        quantisation that makes two models worse

r415 counted that five of eight models in the zoo carry tensors quantised one
scale a row, because llama_auto_kmax pins the group at 1024 and npuquant falls
back to a per-row scale whenever that does not divide a tensor's K.
CHARSIU_NPU_W4_GROUP_FIT, default off, gives each tensor the widest PROPER
divisor of its own K that is no wider than the width asked for, and slices it
at that. This is what it buys and what it costs.

  board     ROCK 4D, boot afe55e04, 594 MHz, boot entry 1, performance
            governor, three readings an arm, medians. binary 95e72869ba97.
  desk      quality, 511 scored positions of tests/corpus/long.txt.

TWO ARE STRICTLY BETTER ON BOTH AXES. Qwen2.5-1.5B gains 27.9% of perplexity
AND 2.2% of decode; gemma-3-1b gains 24.2% and 0.5%. tinyllama gains 5.4% of
quality for 0.3% of decode, which is as near free as this tree measures.

THREE ARE UNCHANGED TO THE DIGIT, WHICH IS THE PROPERTY WORTH HAVING. Llama,
Phi-3.5 and SmolLM2-1.7B read the same perplexity with the flag on as off,
because their K values already have a proper divisor at the requested width and
the fit picks the same number. The flag is a no-op where it is not needed, ON as
well as off.

THEIR -0.4% OF DECODE IS THE FLAG'S OWN COST AND IT IS NOT NOTHING. Nothing
about those three models' weights changes, so that is the doubled slot capacity
the flag allocates, measured. It is the price of having it on at all.

=====================================================================
2. AND TWO GET WORSE FROM A STRICTLY FINER QUANTISATION
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Qwen3-0.6B's only change is its 1024 wide tensors going from one scale a row to
two groups of 512 -- strictly finer, nothing else moves, its n_ff of 3072 was
already three groups at 1024. Perplexity gets WORSE by 1.7%.

gemma-4-E2B's changes are 1536 from one scale a row to three groups of 512 and
256 to two of 128. Also strictly finer. Worse by 12.2%.

THIS IS NOT FLOAT ROUNDING FROM A DIFFERENT SLICE COUNT, which is what r416
found behind KFIT. These numbers are the DESK, where CHARSIU_NPU=0 and there
are no slices: npu_matvec loops the groups and accumulates in double. The
difference is the quantisation itself.

BOTH SIGNS HOLD ON THE SECOND CORPUS, which is what this tree does with a
result it does not like. tests/corpus/long2.txt, same arms:

    Qwen3-0.6B     long  87.8019 -> 89.2738   +1.7%
                   long2 120.4219 -> 124.7481  +3.6%
    Qwen2.5-1.5B   long  37.6358 -> 27.1185  -27.9%
                   long2 51.3478 -> 37.4671  -27.0%

Qwen2.5's gain reproduces to within a point of its own size. Qwen3's loss
reproduces in sign and grows. Neither is one text.

AND IT IS THE THIRD INSTANCE. r415 §6 has gemma-4-E2B at group 512 reading
112.4869 against 89.0370 ungrouped, on two corpora, and could not explain it
either. Four models go the other way and go a long way -- Qwen2.5 by 27.9%,
gemma-3-1b by 24.2%, tinyllama by 5.4%, and r415's whole gemma-3-1b ladder is
monotone from 108.7063 down to 47.0269 -- so a finer group is usually better
and sometimes is not, and nothing here says which.

WHAT IT IS NOT: not the group COUNT (gemma-3-1b's odd rungs are its best), not
AWQ (default 0.0 and no arm set it), not the weight cache (off unless
CHARSIU_NPU_CACHE is set), not the K slicing (the desk has none), and not one
corpus (r415 §6 checked the second one).

WHAT IT IS: WEIGHT ERROR IS NOT FUNCTIONAL ERROR, AGAIN, AND THIS TIME FROM
THE OTHER DIRECTION. CHARSIU_NPU_RMS=1 makes the quantiser report each tensor's
reconstruction error, and with the flag on it falls on every model:

    model          mean rms FIT=0  FIT=1           ppl
    gemma-3-1b        14.2882%   12.4938%  -12.6%   -24.2%   better
    gemma-4-E2B       14.2980%   13.1508%   -8.0%   +12.2%   worse
    Qwen3-0.6B        13.6892%   12.0744%  -11.8%    +1.7%   worse

Qwen3's row is its k=1024 tensors, the only ones the flag touches; its 2048 and
3072 tensors read 14.0054% and 14.2735% either way, which is the flag doing
exactly what it says and nothing else.

EVERY MODEL'S WEIGHT ERROR GOES DOWN AND THE PERPLEXITY GOES BOTH WAYS. It has
to go down: a finer partition of the same row cannot have a worse absmax. The
reductions are all the same size, 8 to 12.6%, while the quality outcome differs
in SIGN. So the Frobenius error cannot predict the direction, let alone the
size.

This tree already has that lesson from the other side -- a reconstruction with
18.3% weight error scored ppl 1701 where Gaussian noise of the same magnitude
scored 32.10, because what matters is the STRUCTURE of the error and Frobenius
barely charges for it. Round 417 is the same statement read backwards: a
genuine reduction in weight error, and two models of eight pay for it.

So this is a known class rather than an unexplained result, and what that rules
out is an implementation fault. It still supplies no rule for which models are
on which side, and nothing here has one.

=====================================================================
3. WHAT THE FLAG DOES NOT DO
---------------------------------------------------------------------
It does not make a narrow group free. One dispatch cannot cover K wider than
one group -- the hardware sums a whole slice into one accumulator per output
channel and the CPU applies that slice's single scale afterwards -- so a
narrower group is more slices, always. The board column above is that cost
already paid.

It does not group everything. gemma-3-1b's o_proj K of 1024 gets 512, its 1152
gets 576 and its 6912 gets 864, which are three different widths on one model
and exactly what a per tensor rule is for; but a tensor whose K is prime, or
whose only proper divisors are tiny, still gets one scale a row.

And it is off. Two of eight models get worse, and picking per model is a
decision about output that belongs to whoever ships the thing, not to a
default that changes under them.
