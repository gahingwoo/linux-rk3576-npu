round 393, 2026-09-13 — both runtimes' TTFT curves, at one NPU clock

r392 measured charsiu's TTFT against prompt length and withdrew the "1.23x per
prompt token" figure that two points a side had produced. This round measures
the vendor's curve so the gap can be stated as something other than unmeasured.

ONE SCRIPT, ONE SET OF PROMPTS. tests/board_ttft_curve.sh grew a vendor arm
rather than a sibling script: it picks the runtime off whichever driver is
bound, so both halves run the same text through the same ladder. A comparison
whose two sides were prompted by two different files is not one.

CONDITIONS  NPU 594 MHz on BOTH sides -- the vendor arm booted from the U-Boot
            menu rather than by editing extlinux.conf, so nothing persistent
            changed -- rail 800 mV, CPU pinned 2016/2208 with the userspace
            governor. 3 readings a point, one warm-up discarded.
            vendor: librkllmrt 1.3.0 via vendor_bench, boot 4a97a58c.
            charsiu: charsiu_run 2026-09-12, -c 1024, boot b5d1c3fc.

=====================================================================
0. A CAP THAT WAS OURS AND READ AS THEIRS
---------------------------------------------------------------------
The first vendor ladder stopped at 485 tokens: 635 came back rc=-1 and the row
said their runtime refused it. It was `p.max_context_len = 512` in our own
vendor_bench.cpp. A limit belongs to whoever set it.

AND IT MADE THE COMPARISON UNFAIR IN A WAY NOBODY WOULD HAVE SEEN. charsiu's
ladder runs -c 1024. One side was allocated half the context of the other for
the whole of the first curve. VENDOR_CTX now defaults to 1024 and matches.

The control, because a re-run at a new setting has to be shown not to move the
points that already worked:

        tokens    ctx 512    ctx 1024     change
            60      414.5       413.2     -0.31%
            85      528.1       529.6     +0.28%
           135      934.4       931.8     -0.28%
           235     1527.4      1528.5     +0.07%
           335     2141.9      2145.0     +0.14%
           485     3194.0      3194.7     +0.02%

THE CONTEXT ALLOCATION DOES NOT MOVE THEIR PREFILL TIMING. Six points inside
0.31%. And this is a null that means something rather than a knob that did
nothing: at 512 the 635 and 885 rows were refusals and at 1024 they run, so the
setting demonstrably reached the runtime.

              60      413.2   412.0..414.1        -
              85      529.6   529.5..529.8        4.656

             885     6026.8  6026.4..6028.8       7.305

1) THEIR MARGINAL IS NON-MONOTONE TOO. 4.656, then 8.044, then 5.967. A
convex function cannot do that, so their prompt length also decides something
discrete -- their own chunking. r392 found the same shape on our side and
attributed it to onechunk_on(); this is the independent half, on a runtime
whose source nobody here has.

2) THEIR CHAT TEMPLATE COSTS EXACTLY 33 TOKENS, AT EVERY LENGTH. 27->60,
52->85, 102->135, 202->235, 302->335, 452->485, 602->635, 852->885. Eight
lengths over a thirty-fold range, the same constant every time.
So the "tokeniser trap" is not two different tokenisers. It is ONE tokenisation
of the body plus a fixed wrapper, which is why the two ways of lining the
curves up differ by exactly that constant and nothing else.

3) THEIR PREFILL USES THE NPU CLOCK, WHICH THEIR DECODE DOES NOT. At 113
tokens this curve interpolates to 727.0 ms at 594 MHz; r391 measured 692.6 ms
at 786 MHz on the same CPU setting. 4.7%, against r390's 6.5% from the same
clock change in a different round. Same sign, same order, two rounds apart.
The two are not the same TEXT at that token count, so this is a
corroboration and not a measurement.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
The two runtimes cannot share a boot -- one arm binds one driver -- so this
comparison breaks the tree's rule against A/B across sessions. The answer is
r390's: measure the drift instead of quoting 3% from memory. The same ladder,
same arm, same binary, one boot apart:

=====================================================================
3. THE TWO CURVES, FITTED
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Least squares a + b*n + c*n^2 on eight points each -- three parameters, five
degrees of freedom, which is a fit and not the arithmetic r392 threw out.

                 fixed a      linear b        quadratic c
    charsiu     187.9 ms   5.456 ms/tok   0.004483 ms/tok^2
    vendor       45.8 ms   6.095 ms/tok   0.000743 ms/tok^2
                   4.1x          0.90x               6.0x

  residual, as a share of each point's own TTFT
    charsiu   27:+2.2  52:-11.5  102:+7.2  202:-1.1  302:+1.0  452:-1.9  602:+1.0  852:-0.1
    vendor    60:-0.2  85: -7.5  135:+5.3  235:+0.6  335:-1.2  485:+0.6  635:-0.4  885:+0.1

Both fit to within 2% beyond 200 tokens. The 5-11% residuals at the short end
are the chunk steps, which a smooth curve cannot follow and which are the
reason the marginal column below is not usable point by point.

5) OUR PER-TOKEN LINEAR RATE IS BETTER THAN THEIRS, BY 12%.
5.456 against 6.095 ms a token. THE WITHDRAWN "the vendor is 1.23x faster
per prompt token" WAS NOT MERELY UNSUPPORTED, IT HAD THE SIGN BACKWARDS. Two
points a side, over two different token ranges, on a curve with a large
quadratic term, produced a number that pointed the wrong way.

6) THEY WIN ON THE OTHER TWO TERMS, AND BOTH ARE LARGE.
Fixed cost 45.8 against our 187.9 ms, a factor of 4.1.
Quadratic 0.000743 against our 0.004483, a factor of 6.0.
The quadratic term is attention. Per prompt token we are ahead; what we lose
is how that cost GROWS, and a fixed cost four times theirs on top.

7) SO THE ANSWER TO "who starts a prompt faster" IS A CROSSOVER, NOT A
RATIO. Solving the two fits with their 33-token template included puts it at
248 of our tokens, 281 of theirs. Measured, we are ahead at 202 and behind at
302, which brackets it.
