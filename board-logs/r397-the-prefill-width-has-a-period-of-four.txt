round 397, 2026-09-13 — the prefill chunk width has a period of 4, and that is
                        the whole of "the one-chunk knob depends on the model"

Evidence pack 1h measured the one-chunk widening at ONE prompt length on ONE
model and found the shipped default 5.9% slower. It went in against 128-token
prompts where it won 5.1 and 5.7%. Two points, opposite signs, and nothing
between them.

=====================================================================
0. FIRST, THE KNOB'S DOMAIN, WHICH NOBODY HAD NAMED
---------------------------------------------------------------------
onechunk_on() widens when `n_ids > chunk && n_ids <= cap`, chunk 80 and cap
163840/widest_k_slice. So it can only ever act on prompts of 81..cap tokens.

AND cap IS PER MODEL. Llama-3.2-1B and Qwen3-0.6B have 160. gemma-3-1b has
**80**, so its condition is `n > 80 && n <= 80` and the knob CANNOT FIRE on it
at any length. Every earlier discussion of this knob treated it as global.

=====================================================================
1. THE FIRST RUN WAS BIASED AND A CONTROL CAUGHT IT
---------------------------------------------------------------------
The sweep runs both arms at each length. The first version ran "on" then "off"
inside every repeat, so the on arm always took the colder of the pair.

gemma-3-1b is the model where the knob cannot act, so both its arms ran
IDENTICAL widths at every length and their ratio had to be 1.000. It read:

    82 tok 1.019   114 tok 1.014   146 tok 1.012
   178 tok 1.037   210 tok 1.038   274 tok 1.036

1.2% rising to 3.8%. That is the order, not the knob, and it was large enough
to account for most of what the first run called a loss on two models.

The control was added to check the knob's DOMAIN and what it caught was the
EXPERIMENT. Alternating the order as well as the arms brings every
identical-width pair to within 1.2%.

=====================================================================
2. WITH THE ORDER ALTERNATING: the knob splits by model
---------------------------------------------------------------------
  model            in band (113/114 tok)   one-chunk width   controls
  Qwen3-0.6B          0.944  knob WINS          1x112        1.002, 0.988
  tinyllama-1.1b      1.069  knob LOSES         1x114        0.998, 0.997
  Llama-3.2-1B        1.074  knob LOSES         1x114        0.993, 0.998
  gemma-3-1b          cannot fire              1x80+tail     1.007, 1.029, 1.022

tinyllama is one of the models the default was FITTED on, where it won 5.7%.
It now loses 6.9%. Several things landed in between -- the MAXN default, the
affinity pin, the QoS hold, the per-layer head_dim -- and "a default fitted
under conditions that changed" is what this tree keeps finding.

=====================================================================
3. THE MECHANISM: THE PERIOD IS 4
---------------------------------------------------------------------
112 and 114 are two tokens apart and the sign reverses. Sweeping the width
itself, prompts built to an exact token count ("A record." is 4 tokens, " cat"
is exactly 1), one chunk, clock pinned, 3 repeats:

  Llama-3.2-1B, ms a token, width in brackets

    [106] 8.274   [108] 7.370   [110] 8.209   [112] 7.571   [114] 8.395
    [116] 7.466   [118] 8.475   [120] 7.492   [122] 8.320   [124] 7.185
    [126] 8.246   [128] 7.797   [130] 8.354   [132] 7.205   [134] 8.343
    [136] 7.324   [138] 8.203   [140] 7.050   [142] 8.345   [144] 7.333
    [146] 8.349   [148] 7.466   [150] 8.480

1) STRICT ALTERNATION, ELEVEN OF ELEVEN. Every width ≡ 0 (mod 4) is cheaper
a token than both its neighbours at ≡ 2. About 11% on this model.

2) AND IT IS NOT 16, WHICH IS WHAT I EXPECTED AND WROTE DOWN FIRST. The
FEATURE ATOM the mesa work landed on is 16, so 112, 128 and 144 were the
predicted dips. 128 reads 7.797 against 7.185 at 124 and 7.205 at 132 -- the
WORST of the three nearest multiples of 4. 16 is not special here; 4 is.

3) REPLICATED ON A SECOND ARCHITECTURE, SIX OF SIX.

  Qwen3-0.6B, ms a token  [106] 6.505  [108] 6.119  [110] 6.459  [112] 6.319
                          [114] 6.722  [116] 6.282  [118] 6.714  [120] 6.264
                          [122] 6.618  [124] 6.016  [126] 6.567  [128] 6.248

  every ≡ 0 below both ≡ 2 neighbours, about 6.3%.

4) AND ON A THIRD, DILUTED EXACTLY AS IT SHOULD BE. gemma-3-1b cannot widen,
so its first chunk is always 80 and only the TAIL varies: 5 of 6 pairs in the
right order and the effect down to about 2%, because the constant 80 carries
most of the work.

5) SO "THE KNOB DEPENDS ON THE MODEL" WAS NEVER ABOUT THE MODEL. Qwen3 wins
because its tokeniser lands the widened width on 112, which is ≡ 0. Llama and
tinyllama lose because theirs lands on 114, which is ≡ 2. One rule, different
residues.

6) THE TREE ALREADY CHOSE 4, FOR A DIFFERENT REASON. prefill_width()'s own
comment prefers "4 + 4 rather than 6 + 2" because "4 is a width the board has
measured EXACT on 225 to 277 real tensors" and 6 has never been run. That was
a correctness argument. It turns out to name the same number as the cost.

=====================================================================
WHAT THIS DOES NOT SUPPORT
   Rounding every chunk down to a multiple of 4. At 114 tokens that is 1x112
   plus two tokens through the token loop, about 940 ms against 957 for
   1x114 -- a 1.8% win -- while simply NOT widening gives 1x80+1x34 at 892 ms,
   which is better than both. The per-token rate is not the whole cost.
   Any claim about widths outside 106..150, or below 80.
   Decode. This is TTFT only.
   One board, one clock, one rail.
