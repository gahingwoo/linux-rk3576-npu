THIS REVERSES WHAT 13/14 SAYS. Its sentence is "the prompt, where the second
core earns its keep". The second core is worth 20 to 31% of DECODE and only 5
to 10% of the prompt. That is what the serialised default predicts once it is
said out loud: batched prefill submits do not overlap, so the second core is
not helping the prompt much; decode is where it is working.

WHAT THIS IS NOT. It is what a CORE is worth at 786 MHz and 800 mV. What
192 MHz is worth is a different quantity and needs the 594 MHz device tree,
which needs a flash. 13/14 put both in one cell and mislabelled the arm:
1037/1565/5073/3604 is the SERIAL default, two cores never at once, not one
core.

=====================================================================
2) DOES THE NPU POWER DOMAIN CYCLE UNDER A REAL WORKLOAD
=====================================================================

Sashiko's open finding on 4/14: pm_runtime_put_autosuspend() is asynchronous
with a 50 ms delay, so a workload whose submit gap is shorter never cycles the
domain, 10/14's power-on reset pulse never fires, and rocket_core_reset()'s own
resets are all that run.

THE COVER ANSWERED THIS WITH THE WRONG INSTRUMENT. "11.4 s active and 17.8 s
suspended in sysfs" came from power/runtime_active_time, which is per DEVICE,
and the objection is about the DOMAIN. That pair also has no artifact anywhere
in this tree; it appears in the cover letter and nowhere else.

rfc-send-v12/sashiko-3-genpd.sh, reading /sys/kernel/debug/pm_genpd/<domain>/,
across one 60.8 s decode of Phi-3.5-mini at 192 tokens:

  domain    active delta   idle delta    idle-state usage delta
  npu         +46777 ms     +14177 ms      +202
  nputop      +46601 ms     +14344 ms      +202
  npu0        +45945 ms     +14983 ms      +210
  npu1        +46017 ms     +14901 ms      +217

  active + idle = 60954 ms against 60800 ms of wall clock, which is the
  instrument checking itself. current_state reads off-0 before and after.

So the domain cycles about two hundred times a minute under a real decode and
the power-on pulse fires. Sashiko's case is still open for the path it is
actually about: whether it cycles after a TIMED-OUT job depends on the gap to
the next submit, and inducing a timeout needs a kernel with JOB_TIMEOUT_MS
lowered and a flash. That half remains an argument, not a measurement.
