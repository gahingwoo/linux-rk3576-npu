round 407, 2026-09-13 — the int8 KV road, and the factor that was holding it up
                        was the OUTPUT WIDTH, not the weight dtype

The handoff that opened this session put the int8 K surface first on measured
grounds: r404's "2.4x on the scores matmul, fence 507 -> 211, saves 296 ms",
scale per position, fixed at append, never repacked. Clean by design.

Three of the four things that sentence rests on are wrong, and the first
board hour says so.

=====================================================================
1. ⛔⛔⛔ THE 2.4x WAS THE OUTPUT WIDTH
---------------------------------------------------------------------
r404 compared three weight dtypes in one probe at one batching, which was the
right instrument and the reason its number was believed. What it did not hold
still is what the DPU WRITES.

`wide8` is the four byte accumulator output stage and job.c gates it:

    if (job->acc_out)        wide8 = 0x3f;
    if (mm->wdtype != INT8)  wide8 = 0;

so for fp16 the wide output comes from `w4_dpu` instead, which is on whatever
acc_out says. r404's fp16 arm wrote FOUR bytes an output and its int8 arm
wrote ONE. At m=78 k=64 n=864 that is 270 kB against 67 kB, and the whole
difference it reported is that.

CHARSIU_WIDE8=0x3f gives the int8 arm the same four byte output. Same binary,
same boot, two passes, us a submit:

    shape             fp16 w    int8 w 1B out    int8 w 4B out
    k=64   n=864     180/166       74/75          160/160
    k=1024 n=64      103/103       71/70           71/70
    k=1024 n=1024    402/409      233/244         281/282

🏁 1) THE SCORES SHAPE IS 1.04x, NOT 2.4x. The weight dtype is worth nothing
there. The K surface -- the one the handoff put FIRST, the clean one, the one
that never needs repacking -- buys about 20 ms of a 7100 ms TTFT.

⭐ 2) AND THE VALUES SHAPE IS REAL. At k=1024 n=64 the narrow and wide int8
arms are the SAME number, 70 and 71 us both ways, because that output is 20 kB
and the width cannot matter. 103/70 = 1.45x with nothing riding along.

⛔ 3) SO THE RISK ORDER IN THE HANDOFF IS EXACTLY INVERTED. It said do K (2.4x,
clean, never repack) and leave V (1.45x, needs a repack). K is worth nothing
and V is the only one worth anything -- and V is the one that needs the repack,
the one whose scale axis is shared across the context, and now also the one
that needs int8 PROBABILITIES, which section 2 explains and which no round has
ever gated for quality.

🔑 4) THE SHAPE OF THE ERROR, AGAIN. A factor attributed to the variable that
was being swept while a second variable moved with it. Same as the 6x quadratic
r405 withdrew, same as "ic > 32" when the rule was "ic a multiple of 16". The
tell was available and cheap: the two arms had different OUTPUT BYTE COUNTS and
nothing printed them.

=====================================================================
2. ⭐ w8a16 DOES NOT EXIST: AN int8 WEIGHT FORCES AN int8 ACTIVATION
---------------------------------------------------------------------
The road assumed w8a16 -- int8 cache, fp16 query -- because the precision
register composes out of two halves that are each confirmed on the vendor's own
file: 0x00000000 for int8 weights, 0x20000000 for a 16 bit activation. Their
SUM appears in zero of its 8308 dispatches. Two confirmed halves are not a
confirmed whole.

npu_fp16_test --w8 ran it and came back with numbers that were not the MAC, not
the MAC of the bytes, and not either of those plus the lift. --w8map decomposes
instead of guessing a fourth time: three weight patterns against four
activations, K=64 N=864, out[0][0] as int32.

                          a=0    a=onehot0    a=onehot1          a=1
    w=0                     0        -7680        -7680      -245760
    w=1                     0        -7620        -7620      -243840
    w=i+1                   0        -7560        -7440      -182400

    fp16 control, same grid, same reader
    w=0                     0            0            0            0
    w=1                     0            1            1           64
    w=i+1                   0            1            2         2080

🏁 5) EVERY CELL IS 60 TIMES THE SIGNED WEIGHT BYTE, and 60 is 0x3c -- the HIGH
BYTE of an fp16 1.0. The hardware is reading the activation buffer as BYTES.

  - a=0 is 0 on every row, so the coefficient record contributes nothing in
    acc_out mode: no bias, no lift.
  - w=0 with one live half gives 60 * (int8)0x80 = -7680.
  - onehot p lands on weight byte 2p+1, so onehot0 -> w=2 gives 60*(-126) and
    onehot1 -> w=4 gives 60*(-124). Both exact.
  - a=1 counts 32 live bytes: the odd byte of each half in the first 64 byte
    positions. 60*(-128)*32 = -245760, and the w=i+1 cell is -182400 = 60 *
    sum(2t+2-128) over t=0..31. Exact.

So 0x20000000 does nothing for an int8 weight. The int8 KV road is w8a8, and
the QUERY has to be quantised too.

⭐ 6) w8a8 IS EXACT. At m=1, K=64, N=864, symmetric int8 both sides: worst
|int32 read - the integer MAC| is 0 over 864 channels, largest |MAC| 29663. At
m>1 the accumulator is not flat -- charsiu_acc_index is that order, and the
probe now reports both readings so it cannot say "wrong" when it means "I
cannot read this".

⛔ 7) AND THE PER OUTPUT CHANNEL SCALE TABLE DOES NOT REACH THE OUTPUT. The two
arms differ only in job.weight_scales and their outputs are identical to the
last digit: -26488 21836 23136 -29003 both ways, against a wanted -104.283
111.759 145.739 -216.952. In acc_out mode the answer is the raw accumulator and
the requant, which is where that table lives, is bypassed.

That is survivable -- the scores scale is per position and the softmax already
multiplies every element by a row scale, so it is one extra load and multiply
an element -- but it is a different patch from the one the handoff described,
and it had been assumed rather than checked. The hardware having a per channel
table (r406, and job.weight_scales landed for it) is true; the acc_out path
reading it is not.

3. ⛔ THE SCORES MATMUL WAS ALREADY A TRIANGLE
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Section 1 leaves the cost model: holding m and k still and moving only n, at
m=78 k=64 fp16, us a submit --

    n     864    432    224    112
    us    174     94     61     42          about 22 + 0.176*n

-- so three quarters of the scores fence is the output write and the weight
fetch, both linear in n. Which raised the obvious idea: a chunk of rows
starting at p0 attends to nothing after its own last row, so the columns past
p0+m are computed, written, and then memset by the values pack. xtri0 has been
telling the pack that since r400 and the scores matmul did not know.

Built it (CHARSIU_ATTN_NPU_CAUSAL_N, both arms named). Four lengths, arms
alternating, one warm-up discarded:

    tokens   rect ms  scores ms    tri ms  scores ms   TTFT t/r
       102       929         19       928         19      0.999
       302      2380         70      2344         70      0.985
       602      4918        216      4886        216      0.993
       852      7105        385      7102        385      1.000

    the same tokens out of both arms at 852: identical, 8b02145bf030

⛔ 8) ZERO, AND THE REASON IS IN THE FUNCTION. T is pos0 + j->n -- the BLOCK's
own last position, not the prompt's -- so npad was already the block's causal
width. What this narrows is the rb sub-loop, and that runs once:
llama_prefill_chunk_cap is 163840/1024 = 160 here and a->mmax is 5120/(kv/32) =
160 too.

⛔ 9) AND SUB-CHUNKING TO MAKE IT LIVE IS A NET LOSS, by arithmetic and not by
another round. Splitting the 160 row block in two takes the columns computed at
852 tokens from 537600 to 499200 -- 7% -- while doubling the task count, which
r403 priced at 0.27 ms a row a doubling, 10% of attention.

Kept, default on, because it is never MORE work and it pays if a->mmax falls
below the chunk cap, which a longer context does. The zero is in the comment.

🏁 10) THE SINGLE LARGEST LINE IN THE PREFILL IS THE READBACK OF THE int4
ACCUMULATOR: 2.14 ms a row is 1823 ms, 26% of the whole prompt, and it is
LARGER THAN THE FENCE it waits on (1.78) and larger than attention's two fences
put together (889).

It is a permuted gather -- charsiu_acc_index's order, through a table of one
entry per four channels -- moving about 2.5 GB at roughly 1.4 GB/s.

⚠ 11) AND THE TREE HAS ALREADY CLOSED THE TWO OBVIOUS DOORS, with measurements
in the code: pooling it lost twice (283 -> 463 ms and 241 -> 430, the same
190 ms both times, which is the barrier and not the work), READ4 is 2.3x slower
on eight models, and READ_FUSE is 2.3% slower. The note ends "the read is at its
floor FOR THIS ACCUMULATOR LAYOUT".

That qualifier is the whole remaining lead. The layout is the one variable
nobody has moved. The fp16 path at the same shapes reads FLAT and copies
nothing at all -- charsiu_fp16_out_w hands back a pointer -- and it differs
from the int4 path in the M axis (height against width) and in DPU 0x4028,
which fp16 writes as n/4-1 and every other dtype writes as 0.

⚠ NOT SETTLED HERE, and the one cheap arm that looked like it would settle it
does not: with CHARSIU_M_AXIS=w the fp16 job writes NOTHING (every output still
0xdeadbeef, which reads as -6.26e18 and is what that number is in this round's
notes), and at m=2 n=864 neither axis writes. That is the shape wedge this file
already carries a warning about, not an answer about ordering.

=====================================================================
5. WHAT THIS ROUND LEAVES
---------------------------------------------------------------------
⛔ int8 KV is not the road. K is 1.04x. V is 1.45x and worth about 120 ms, and
   it now costs an int8 V surface with a repack on every ladder growth, int8
   PROBABILITIES whose quality nothing has ever gated, a CPU side per channel
   scale because acc_out ignores the table, and the m>1 accumulator order.
   120 ms of 7100 for all of that.

⭐ The named target is the int4 readback, 1823 ms, and the question is whether
   that accumulator can be written flat. It needs a probe on the int4 path of
   the kind npu_fp16_test --outmap is for fp16 -- a known answer at m>1, read
   both ways -- and then, if it is the M axis or 0x4028, a stream change.

🔑 12) THREE OF THE FOUR CLAIMS THAT OPENED THIS SESSION CAME FROM ONE PROBE
THAT CHECKS NOTHING. --cost's header says so in capitals and it was still read
as a dtype factor, a design, and a projection to a TTFT. A timing probe with no
oracle can only ever tell you what a dispatch COSTS -- and only if every other
thing about the dispatch is held still, which is the part that went wrong.
