1) MONOTONE IN head_dim, ACROSS SEVEN MODELS AND FIVE ARCHITECTURES.
1.302, 1.213, 1.021, 0.877. Nothing out of order.

2) THE TWO head_dim 128 MODELS ARE A REPLICATE AND THEY AGREE TO 0.5%.
Qwen2.5-1.5B reads 1.023 and Qwen3-0.6B 1.018 at 852 tokens -- different
architectures, different layer counts, different sizes. That is the strongest
single piece of evidence on this axis and it was the point of running both.

3) AND IT SHOWS THE WITHDRAWN RULE'S THRESHOLD IS TOO LOOSE. "head_dim >=
128" would enable this arm on Qwen2.5 and Qwen3, where it BREAKS EVEN at the
longest prompt measured and loses at every shorter one. The threshold for a WIN
is 256, not 128 -- and even there it needs about 230 tokens.

4) WHICH IS SUPPORTED BY EXACTLY ONE MODEL. gemma-3-1b is the only clean
head_dim 256 point on the card. So the default still does not move: n=1 for
the only case that wins.

5) AND THAT IS THE ARGUMENT FOR WIDENING THE MIRROR'S SINGLE head_dim.

WHICH IS A TRADE-OFF AND NOT A BUG, and r394 was written as though it might
be one. The tree knows gemma-4 has two head_dims and says so BY NAME:
attn_block_job carries `hd`, this layer's head, and `hdmax`, the cache's
stride, because "on gemma4 they differ ... Indexing the cache by the live head
would make layer 4 read where layer 0 wrote". The CPU path handles both
correctly. The fp16 mirror deliberately refuses the mismatching layers rather
than, in its own words, squeezing them in beside. What r394 and r395 add is the
COST of that choice, which nothing had measured.

gemma-4-E2B's 28 sliding-window layers are head_dim 256 -- the winning value --
and they cannot enter the pool, which holds one head_dim (r394). Letting it
hold two would do two things at once: take gemma-4 from 20% of its attention
layers accelerated to 100%, and give this axis its SECOND head_dim 256 point,
which is the one thing it is short of.
