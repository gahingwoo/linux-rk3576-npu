round 390, 2026-09-12 — does the charsiu/vendor ratio depend on the clock?

r389 measured their runtime against ours at 594 MHz and got 1.44x on decode.
A single ratio is only worth quoting if it is a constant. It is not.

TO MEASURE THIS AT ALL, KILN'S DRIVER HAD TO STOP PINNING THE CLOCK.
rknpu_drv.c carried a hardcoded clk_set_rate(clks[0], 594000000) with a comment
explaining that devfreq is off and nothing else sets the compute clock. That is
correct as a bring-up default and fatal as an experiment: the vendor arm and
the charsiu arm would have been two different frequencies whenever the charsiu
DTB pinned anything else. It reads assigned-clock-rates from the device tree
now and falls back to 594.

CONDITIONS  one Image, one board, rail 800 mV in every arm, same prompt,
            -n 64, ignore-eos, context 512. Vendor figures from their own
            RKLLMPerfStat; charsiu's from r388, same protocol, same board.

=====================================================================

              594 MHz   786 MHz    change     spread at 786
  charsiu       17.85     18.60     +4.2%     18.51..18.63
  vendor        12.85     12.73     -0.9%     12.72..12.76
  ratio         1.389     1.461     +5.2%

  charsiu TTFT    267       263     -1.5%
  vendor  TTFT    467       437     -6.5%

  the NPU clock itself rose 32.4%.

1) THE RATIO IS NOT A CONSTANT. 1.389 at 594 and 1.461 at 786, a 5.2% move,
which is far outside either arm's spread (0.3% and 0.6%). The paper cannot
quote a single multiple for decode any more than it can for the quality ladder.
Same shape of error, found twice in one day.

2) THEIR DECODE DOES NOT USE THE NPU CLOCK AT ALL. A 32.4% clock increase
buys them -0.9%, with a 0.3% spread, so it is not noise. Ours buys +4.2%, small
but the right sign. Whatever bounds their decode, it is not this clock.

That bears directly on the fairness argument for section 1 of the evidence
pack. Their published figures are "at the maximum CPU and NPU frequencies", and
the worry has always been that our arms run slower than the conditions they
published. On DECODE that worry is much smaller than it looked: the NPU clock
is nearly inert for them.

AND THE OBVIOUS EXTENSION IS NOT SUPPORTED. Only the NPU clock was varied.
"Maximum frequency" in their header is CPU *and* NPU. A decode that ignores the
NPU clock is a decode that may well be bound by the CPU, which is exactly the
thing this round did not touch. Do not read this as "their published figures
would be reproduced at 594 MHz".

3) THE TWO METRICS SWAP SIDES. The clock is nearly inert for their decode
and worth 6.5% of their prefill; for charsiu it is worth 4.2% of decode and
almost nothing on prefill. So the answer to "does frequency favour one of us"
depends on which half is being asked about, and the two halves point opposite
ways.

Not measured here: CPU frequency, either side. Devfreq, either side -- their
device tree asks for it over an OPP table to 800 MHz and Kiln's patch has
always pinned instead, so neither arm has ever scaled dynamically. One board.
