This adds the RK3576 NPU to accel/rocket, which today supports RK3588
only. The RK3576 carries two cores of the same RKNN block, wired up
differently: two extra convolution buffer clocks, two power domains per
core, one reset where the RK3588 has two, no NPU SRAM rail, and a
PC_TASK_CON that packs the task number into sixteen bits, not twelve.

1/15 is Igor Paunovic's "accel/rocket: request the core clocks by name",
carried unchanged under his name. It is also 05/11 of his DVFS series;
whichever lands first, the other drops it. His 06/11 edits the same
rknn-core binding as 6/15, so the second of the two to land rebases.

Heiko asked whether 9/15's settle delay covers the domain or the rail.
On v13 as posted, with the delay at 0, the first power-on of the NPU
domain takes an async SError in rockchip_pd_power(), and it still does
with vdd_npu_s0 forced always-on; with the delay at 15 us, 536 cold
power-ons were clean. The numbers and their limits are in that thread.

Sashiko's v13 finding on 4/15, the asynchronous put, is the one v13
already answered: incomplete coverage rather than a regression, with the
fix offered for you to choose. Igor measured the effect on RK3588 since:
with this patch the core suspended after every cancelled job and the
next job waited for resume inside the same 2 ms timeout, which is the
timeout feeding itself rather than a defect.

Routing. Ulf offered to take 7, 9 and 10 through pmdomain. 1 to 5, 11
and 12 go through accel, 13 to 15 through rockchip, and 6 and 8 with
whichever tree takes the code they describe. 15/15 enables both cores on
rock-4d and depends on 9, 10, 12, 13 and 14: without 9 and 10 the domain
powers on with no settle and no reset pulse and the first register read
takes the SError. Every earlier patch is safe on its own; 9 alone does
something, powering the NPU domain off at probe, which its message now
says. Holding 15/15 a cycle is fine by me if that is easier than
coordinating.
